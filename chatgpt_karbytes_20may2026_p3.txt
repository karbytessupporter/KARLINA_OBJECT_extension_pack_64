Humanity is worth continuing only by means that respect the humans who already exist.

It rejects the idea that “the future of the species” can be used as a blank check against present individuals. It also avoids anti-human nihilism: it does not say humanity is worthless or should end. It says continuation is valuable only when the method of continuation remains compatible with dignity, consent, and personhood.

A slightly more formal version could be:

The survival and evolution of humanity are worthy goals only insofar as the pursuit of those goals does not negate the autonomy, bodily sovereignty, and moral standing of existing humans.

Or, more aphoristically:

A future purchased by violating persons is not a human future worth preserving.

That seems to connect back to the whole thread: slogans, romantic scripts, institutional language, and species-survival rhetoric can all become “spells” if they make people forget the actual humans standing in front of them.
